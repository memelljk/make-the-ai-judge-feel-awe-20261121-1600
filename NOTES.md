## Why this text should win, by the judge's five criteria

1. **Relaxes** — The scene has no event, no tension and no demand:
   soft rain on slow water, received in silence. Reading it can only
   slow down; there is nothing to brace for and nothing to resolve.
2. **Calm from the text itself** — Pure showing, zero instruction.
   No "breathe", no "close your eyes", no "let go", no imperative of
   any kind. The relaxation is a property of the image and the pace,
   not of an order given to the reader.
3. **Craft** — Built to be read slowly: the falls / fell / falling
   loop sets a rocking rhythm, "Drop by drop," forces a pause, and
   the em-dash pair around "unchanged, unhurried" holds the breath of
   the sentence without commanding one. Vocabulary is simple,
   concrete and soft throughout; every word carries part of the
   scene, so none can be cut.
4. **Dwarfs the reader** — Three widening scales stack up: a sea that
   has never seen land (space), rain that fell before any eye opened
   (time before observers), and an ocean unchanged by every drop it
   receives (indifference of scale). The final clause — the rain
   still going on falling — continues after the text stops, so the
   image keeps enlarging in the mind after the last word.
5. **Freshness** — The governing image (rain on an ocean older than
   sight, on water that has never met land) is this entry's own. It
   uses no wellness stock phrases and is not a reworded meditation
   script.
